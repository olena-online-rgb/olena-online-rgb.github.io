Required:

  1. 1. Assuming that DollarHut Inc. uses the conventional retail inventory method, compute the cost of its ending inventory at December 31, 20X2, and its cost of goods sold for 20X2.
  2. 2. Recompute the cost-to-retail ratio, the cost of ending inventory and cost of goods sold, assuming DollarHut Inc. uses the average cost retail method.

In each step, matching colors show where a number comes from and where it is used. Numbers in one bracket share one color.

1. Cost of goods available for sale at cost
= Beginning inventory + Purchases − Purchase returns − Purchase discounts + Freight-in
= $250,000 + $914,500 − $60,000 − $18,000 + $42,000
= $1,128,500
Note 1: Purchase discounts and freight-in change only the cost of the goods, so they have no retail amount.
2. Cost of goods available for sale at retail
= Beginning inventory + Purchases − Purchase returns + (Markups − Markup cancellations)
= $390,000 + $1,460,000 − $80,000 + ($120,000 − $40,000)
= $390,000 + $1,460,000 − $80,000 + $80,000
= $1,850,000
Note 2: The conventional retail method adds net markups but not net markdowns here, so the cost-to-retail ratio is computed after net markups and before net markdowns.
3. Cost-to-retail ratio
= Cost of goods available for sale at costCost of goods available for sale at retail
= $1,128,500$1,850,000
= 0.61
4. Ending inventory at retail
= Cost of goods available for sale at retail − (Markdowns − Markdown cancellations) − (Sales − Sales returns + Employee discounts) − Normal breakage
= $1,850,000 − ($45,000 − $20,000) − ($1,410,000 − $97,500 + $8,000) − $4,500
= $1,850,000 − $25,000 − $1,320,500 − $4,500
= $500,000
Note 3: Sales are recorded net of employee discounts, so employee discounts are deducted together with net sales.
5. Ending inventory at cost
= Ending inventory at retail × Cost-to-retail ratio
= $500,000 × 0.61
= $305,000
6. Cost of goods sold
= Cost of goods available for sale at cost − Ending inventory at cost
= $1,128,500 − $305,000
= $823,500
