Cost Flow Assumptions

Evening Star Company is a multiproduct firm with a fiscal year ending December 31. Presented below is information concerning one of its products, the Lantern, for 20X1.

DateTransactionQuantityPrice/Cost
1/1Beginning inventory800$20
3/5Purchase1,200$25
4/18Sale1,500$40
7/9Purchase2,000$30
10/22Sale1,800$45

Required: compute cost of goods sold and ending inventory, assuming Evening Star uses:

  1. (a) Periodic system, FIFO cost flow.
  2. (b) Perpetual system, FIFO cost flow.
  3. (c) Periodic system, LIFO cost flow.
  4. (d) Perpetual system, LIFO cost flow.
  5. (e) Periodic system, weighted average cost flow.
  6. (f) Perpetual system, moving average cost flow.

Matching colors show where a number comes from and where it is used.Used in this step

Work setup

Cost of goods available for sale
= 800 units × $20 + 1,200 units × $25 + 2,000 units × $30
= $16,000 + $30,000 + $60,000 = $106,000
Units available for sale
= 800 + 1,200 + 2,000 = 4,000 units
Units sold
= 1,500 + 1,800 = 3,300 units
Units in ending inventory
= 4,000 − 3,300 = 700 units
Ending inventory
= Units in ending inventory × Cost of the latest purchase (7/9)
= 700 units × $30 = $21,000
Note: Since 700 units < 2,000 units bought on 7/9, all 700 units in ending inventory come from the 7/9 purchase.
Cost of goods sold
= Cost of goods available for sale − Ending inventory
= $106,000 − $21,000 = $85,000
Cost of goods sold
= Units sold, oldest layers first
= 800 units × $20 + 1,200 units × $25 + 1,300 units × $30
= $16,000 + $30,000 + $39,000 = $85,000
Note: 3,300 − 800 − 1,200 = 1,300 units sold from the 7/9 purchase.
Ending inventory
= Cost of goods available for sale − Cost of goods sold
= $106,000 − $85,000 = $21,000
Same answers as periodic FIFO (a)
Note: FIFO always sells the oldest units first, so costing each sale on its date sends out the same units as costing all 3,300 at year-end. Periodic FIFO (a) can be solved this way too, if that is more convenient.
Ending inventory
= Units in ending inventory × Cost of beginning inventory (1/1)
= 700 units × $20 = $14,000
Note: Since 700 units < 800 units in 1/1 inventory, all 700 units left at year-end cost $20 each.
Cost of goods sold
= Cost of goods available for sale − Ending inventory
= $106,000 − $14,000 = $92,000
Or, cost of goods sold directly
= Units sold, newest layers first
= 2,000 units × $30 + 1,200 units × $25 + 100 units × $20
= $60,000 + $30,000 + $2,000 = $92,000
Note: 3,300 − 2,000 − 1,200 = 100 units sold from 1/1 inventory.
Cost of the 4/18 sale (1,500 units)
= Newest units on hand on 4/18 first
= 1,200 units × $25 + 300 units × $20
= $30,000 + $6,000 = $36,000
Note: On 4/18, the 7/9 purchase has not happened yet, so the newest units on hand are the 1,200 units bought on 3/5. 1,500 − 1,200 = 300 units come from 1/1 inventory.
Cost of the 10/22 sale (1,800 units)
= Newest units on hand on 10/22 first
= 1,800 units × $30 = $54,000
Note: Since 1,800 units < 2,000 units bought on 7/9, all 1,800 units sold on 10/22 come from the 7/9 purchase.
Cost of goods sold
= Cost of the 4/18 sale + Cost of the 10/22 sale
= $36,000 + $54,000 = $90,000
Ending inventory
= Cost of goods available for sale − Cost of goods sold
= $106,000 − $90,000 = $16,000
Cost per unit (weighted average)
= Cost of goods available for sale ÷ Units available for sale
= $106,0004,000 units = $26.50
Cost of goods sold
= Units sold × Cost per unit
= 3,300 units × $26.50 = $87,450
Or, ending inventory first
Ending inventory = Units in ending inventory × Cost per unit
= 700 units × $26.50 = $18,550
Cost of goods sold = Cost of goods available for sale − Ending inventory
= $106,000 − $18,550 = $87,450
Cost of goods available for sale, before the 4/18 sale
= 800 units × $20 + 1,200 units × $25
= $16,000 + $30,000 = $46,000
Units on hand = 800 + 1,200 = 2,000 units
Average cost per unit on 4/18
= $46,0002,000 units = $23.00
Cost of the 4/18 sale
= 1,500 units × $23.00 = $34,500
Note: 2,000 − 1,500 = 500 units left after the 4/18 sale, still at $23.00 each.
Cost of goods available for sale, before the 10/22 sale
= 500 units × $23.00 + 2,000 units × $30
= $11,500 + $60,000 = $71,500
Units on hand = 500 + 2,000 = 2,500 units
Average cost per unit on 10/22
= $71,5002,500 units = $28.60
Cost of the 10/22 sale
= 1,800 units × $28.60 = $51,480
Cost of goods sold
= Cost of the 4/18 sale + Cost of the 10/22 sale
= $34,500 + $51,480 = $85,980
Ending inventory
= Units in ending inventory × Latest average cost per unit
= 700 units × $28.60 = $20,020
