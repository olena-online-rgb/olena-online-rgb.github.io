1. A single sum

A customer bought Harvelle’s old walk-in cooler and will pay $25,000 four years from today. The market rate is 6%. What is the payment worth today?

With the tables

PV of $1, n = 4, i = 6%25,000 × 0.79209 = 19,802.25

With a BA II Plus

4N6I/Y0PMT25,000FVCPTPV
PV = −19,802.34

Tables 19,802.25; calculator 19,802.34; difference 0.09.

2. An ordinary annuity

Harvelle’s rents its back lot to a food truck for $8,000 a year, received at the end of each year for five years. The market rate is 10%. What are the rent payments worth today?

With the tables

PV of an ordinary annuity of $1, n = 5, i = 10%8,000 × 3.79079 = 30,326.32

With a BA II Plus

5N10I/Y8,000PMT0FVCPTPV
PV = −30,326.29

Tables 30,326.32; calculator 30,326.29; difference 0.03.

3. An interest-bearing note

Harvelle’s lends cash to a local bakery and receives a $50,000, three-year note with an 8% stated rate; interest is received at the end of each year. The market rate is 10%. How much cash does Harvelle’s lend?

With the tables

Face: PV of $1, n = 3, i = 10%50,000 × 0.75131 = 37,565.50

Interest: PV of an ordinary annuity of $1, n = 3, i = 10%4,000 × 2.48685 = 9,947.40

Cash lent37,565.50 + 9,947.40 = 47,512.90

With a BA II Plus

3N10I/Y4,000PMT50,000FVCPTPV
PV = −47,513.15

Tables 47,512.90; calculator 47,513.15; difference 0.25.

Harvelle’s lends cash to a jukebox repair shop and receives a $40,000, three-year, zero-interest-bearing note. The market rate is 9%. How much cash does Harvelle’s lend?

With the tables

PV of $1, n = 3, i = 9%40,000 × 0.77218 = 30,887.20

With a BA II Plus
