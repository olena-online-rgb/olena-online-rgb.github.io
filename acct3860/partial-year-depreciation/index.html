Halo Mutual Insurance: Partial-Year Depreciation

Example 5. DO ON YOUR OWN. On March 31, 20X1, Halo Mutual Insurance purchased an office building for $12,000,000. Based on their relative fair values, one-third of the purchase price was allocated to the land and two-thirds to the building. Furniture and fixtures were purchased separately from office equipment on the same date for $1,200,000 and $700,000, respectively. The company uses the straight-line method to depreciate its buildings and the double-declining-balance method to depreciate all other depreciable assets. The estimated useful lives and residual values of these assets are as follows:

Asset Service Life Residual Value Method Cost
Building3010% of costSL$8,000,000
Furniture and fixtures1010% of costDDB$1,200,000
Office equipment5$30,000DDB$700,000

Required: Calculate depreciation for 20X1 and 20X2. (Do not round intermediate calculations.)

Matching colors show where a number comes from and where it is used.

Building cost
= Purchase price × Share allocated to the building
= $12,000,000 × 2/3
= $8,000,000
Note 1: The other one-third, $12,000,000 × 1/3 = $4,000,000, is the cost of the land. Land is not depreciated.

Note 2: Double-declining balance leaves residual value out of the computation. Residual value only sets a floor: book value may not fall below it.

Note 3: The assets are purchased on March 31, 20X1, so 20X1 depreciation covers April 1 through December 31: 9 of 12 months. 20X2 is a full year of 12 months.

20X1 depreciation expense
= Cost − Residual valueService life × Months in 20X112
= $8,000,000 − $8,000,000 × 10%30 × 9/12
= $8,000,000 − $800,00030 × 9/12
= $7,200,00030 × 9/12
= $240,000 × 9/12
= $180,000
Note 1: Residual value = 10% of cost = $8,000,000 × 10% = $800,000.
20X2 depreciation expense
= Cost − Residual valueService life
= $8,000,000 − $8,000,000 × 10%30
= $8,000,000 − $800,00030
= $7,200,00030
= $240,000
Note 2: 20X2 is a full year, so no fraction of the year is applied. Straight-line depreciation is $240,000 in every full year.
20X1 depreciation expense
= Book value at the beginning of 20X1Service life × 2 × Months in 20X112
= $1,200,00010 × 2 × 9/12
= $120,000 × 2 × 9/12
= $240,000 × 9/12
= $180,000
Note 1: No depreciation has been taken before 20X1, so book value at the beginning of 20X1 is the cost, $1,200,000.
Note 2: $1,200,000 ÷ 10 = $120,000 is one full year of straight-line depreciation. × 2 doubles it, which gives the method its name.
20X2 depreciation expense
= Book value at the beginning of 20X2Service life × 2
= $1,200,000 − $180,00010 × 2
= $1,020,00010 × 2
= $102,000 × 2
= $204,000
Note 3: Book value at the beginning of 20X2 = cost − 20X1 depreciation. 20X2 is a full year, so no fraction of the year is applied.
Note 4: Book value at the end of 20X2 = $1,020,000 − $204,000 = $816,000. It stays above the residual value of $1,200,000 × 10% = $120,000, so the full $204,000 is taken.
20X1 depreciation expense
= Book value at the beginning of 20X1Service life × 2 × Months in 20X112
= $700,0005 × 2 × 9/12
= $140,000 × 2 × 9/12
= $280,000 × 9/12
= $210,000
Note 1: No depreciation has been taken before 20X1, so book value at the beginning of 20X1 is the cost, $700,000.
Note 2: $700,000 ÷ 5 = $140,000 is one full year of straight-line depreciation. × 2 doubles it, which gives the method its name.
20X2 depreciation expense
= Book value at the beginning of 20X2Service life × 2
= $700,000 − $210,0005 × 2
= $490,0005 × 2
= $98,000 × 2
= $196,000
Note 3: Book value at the beginning of 20X2 = cost − 20X1 depreciation. 20X2 is a full year, so no fraction of the year is applied.
Note 4: Book value at the end of 20X2 = $490,000 − $196,000 = $294,000. It stays above the $30,000 residual value, so the full $196,000 is taken.
