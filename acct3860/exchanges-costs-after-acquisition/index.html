Exchanges of Plant Assets and Costs After Acquisition

Example 1. Bobby Corporation traded a used truck (cost $20,000, accumulated depreciation $18,000) for a small computer worth $3,300. Bobby also paid $500 in the transaction. Prepare the journal entry to record the exchange. (The exchange has commercial substance.)

Example 2. Use the information for Bobby Corporation from Example 1. Prepare the journal entry to record the exchange, assuming the exchange lacks commercial substance.

From Example 1: Bobby Corporation traded a used truck (cost $20,000, accumulated depreciation $18,000) for a small computer worth $3,300. Bobby also paid $500 in the transaction.

Example 3. Ellen Company traded a used welding machine (cost $9,000, accumulated depreciation $3,000) for office equipment with an estimated fair value of $5,000. Ellen also paid $3,000 cash in the transaction. Prepare the journal entry to record the exchange. (The exchange has commercial substance.)

Example 4. Jody Company traded a used truck for a new truck. The used truck cost $30,000 and has accumulated depreciation of $27,000. The new truck is worth $37,000. Jody also made a cash payment of $36,000. Prepare Jody’s entry to record the exchange. (The exchange lacks commercial substance.)

Example 5. Charlie Corporation traded a used truck for a new truck. The used truck cost $20,000 and has accumulated depreciation of $17,000. The new truck is worth $35,000. Charlie also made a cash payment of $33,000. Prepare Charlie’s entry to record the exchange. (The exchange has commercial substance.)

Example 6. Indicate which of the following costs should be expensed when incurred.

  1. capitalized (a) $13,000 paid to rearrange and reinstall machinery.
  2. capitalized (b) $200,000 paid for addition to building.
  3. expensed (c) $200 paid for tune-up and oil change on delivery truck.
  4. capitalized (d) $7,000 paid to replace a wooden floor with a concrete floor.
  5. capitalized (e) $2,000 paid for a major overhaul on a truck, which extends the useful life.

Matching colors show where a number comes from and where it is used.

(a) Capitalized
Note 1: Rearranging and reinstalling machinery benefits future periods, so the cost is capitalized.
(b) Capitalized
Note 2: An addition is new building space that will be used for many future periods, so its cost is capitalized to Buildings.
(c) Expensed
Note 3: A tune-up and an oil change are ordinary repairs and maintenance. They do not extend the truck’s useful life or improve the truck, so their cost is expensed when incurred.
(d) Capitalized
Note 4: Replacing the wooden floor with a concrete floor is an improvement, so the cost is capitalized.
(e) Capitalized
Note 5: A major overhaul that extends the useful life benefits future periods, so the cost is capitalized.
