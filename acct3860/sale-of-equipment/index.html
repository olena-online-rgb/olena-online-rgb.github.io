Trench Coat Manufacturing: Sale of Equipment, Straight-Line and Double-Declining-Balance

Trench Coat Manufacturing Company purchased equipment on June 30, 20X1, at a cost of $800,000. The residual value of the equipment was estimated to be $50,000 at the end of a five-year life. The equipment was sold on March 31, 20X5, for $170,000. Trench Coat Manufacturing uses the straight-line depreciation method for all of its plant and equipment. Partial-year depreciation is calculated based on the number of months the asset is in service.

Required:

  1. 1. Prepare the journal entry to record the sale.
  2. 2. Assuming that Trench Coat Manufacturing had instead used the double-declining-balance method, prepare the journal entry to record the sale.

Matching colors show where a number comes from and where it is used.

Months the equipment was in service
= Months in 20X1 + Months in 20X2 + Months in 20X3 + Months in 20X4 + Months in 20X5
= 6 + 12 + 12 + 12 + 3
= 45 months
Note 1: The equipment was purchased on June 30, 20X1, so depreciation in 20X1 runs from July 1 to December 31, 6 months. The equipment was sold on March 31, 20X5, so depreciation in 20X5 runs from January 1 to March 31, 3 months.
Accumulated depreciation, March 31, 20X5
= Cost − Residual valueUseful life × Months in service12
= $800,000 − $50,0005 × 4512
= $750,0005 × 4512
= $150,000 × 4512
= $562,500
Note 2: Accumulated depreciation is brought up to the date of the sale, March 31, 20X5, before the equipment is removed from the books.
Gain or loss on sale
= Cash received − (Cost − Accumulated depreciation)
= $170,000 − ($800,000 − $562,500)
= $170,000 − $237,500
= ($67,500) loss
Note 3: Cash received is less than the book value of the equipment, so the sale results in a loss.
