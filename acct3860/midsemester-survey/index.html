Key open: 48.8% lower vs 26.8% higher

When the key was open along the way, almost twice as many of you scored lower on Exam 1 than higher. Checking the key as you go fixes answers before you grade them, so the practice score likely comes out higher than what you can do during the actual test.

One sitting: 23.1% lower vs 38.5% higher

When the practice exam was taken in one sitting without the key, more of you scored higher than lower. That practice score was a more honest read of where you stood, or perhaps even a little modest.

A caution about the numbers. Only 13 students took the practice exam all at once. With a group that small, the difference between the two rows is not statistically significant (Fisher's exact test, p = 0.12), so it cannot support a strong conclusion. And because you chose how to take it, there is no way to compare how your exam scores would compare for two different ways of taking practice exams. In other words, the comparisons are not as clean as we would like in statistical analyses. Read it as a pattern worth paying attention to, not as proof.

What isn’t working and what should change

Thank you for your honest feedback. Here are your top themes and my responses.

Lecture pace is too fast20 answers
Thank you for telling me. I hear you, and I’m working on it. I’m confident I can slow things down by covering a little less at a time, explaining in simpler terms, and pausing at the right moments in each problem.
Copying notes leaves no room to understand10 answers
It may help to skim the packet before class so the layout feels familiar. During an example, you might try watching the logic first and writing second. I’ll pause at the end of each example so you can catch up, and I’m always happy to fill in any gaps during student hours.
More explanation of concepts and the why6 answers
I’m happy to follow your lead. Please don’t hesitate to stop me and ask for more explanation during lecture. If you’re wondering about something, chances are someone else is too.
More practice problems and study materials6 answers
There may be more available than it seems: SmartBook, the Canvas web pages, the in-class examples to redo on your own, the practice exam, and the review session. One approach that helps: redo the examples without looking, and when you miss one, work out what went wrong, why, and what makes it right.
Review session and exam scheduling4 answers
Unfortunately, exam dates can’t be moved, but the review session for Exam 3 might be. I’ll share a quick survey in class so we can find a time that works for more of you.
Notes and project on paper; use Excel or iPad3 answers
You’re welcome to annotate the PDF notes with a stylus or Apple Pencil if that works better for you. Thank you for the advice about the project, too. I’ll keep it in mind for the next class.
