Inventory Errors

Example 8. DO ON YOUR OWN. For each of the following inventory errors occurring in 20X1, determine the effect of the error on 20X1’s cost of goods sold, net income, and retained earnings using understated (U), overstated (O), or no effect (NE). Assume that the error is not discovered until 20X2 and that a periodic inventory system is used. Ignore income taxes.

ErrorCost of goods soldNet incomeRetained earnings
1. Overstatement of ending inventoryUOO
2. Overstatement of purchasesOUU
3. Understatement of beginning inventoryUONE
4. Freight-in charges are understatedUOO
5. Understatement of ending inventoryOUU
6. Understatement of purchasesUOO
7. Overstatement of beginning inventoryOUNE
8. Understatement of purchases plus understatement of ending inventory by the same amountNENENE

↑ overstated (O)↓ understated (U)

1. Overstatement of ending inventory

Cost of goods sold
Beginning inventory + Purchases + Freight-in − Purchase returns and allowances − Cost of goods sold = Ending inventory
Cost of goods sold↓ = Beginning inventory + Purchases + Freight-in − Purchase returns and allowances − Ending inventory↑
Note 1: Ending inventory is subtracted, so an overstated ending inventory makes cost of goods sold understated.
Net income
Net income↑ = Sales − Cost of goods sold↓ − Other expenses
Note 2: Cost of goods sold is subtracted, so an understated cost of goods sold makes net income overstated.
Retained earnings
12/31/20X1 retained earnings↑ = 1/1/20X1 retained earnings + Net income↑ − Dividends
Note 3: Net income is added, so an overstated net income makes 12/31/20X1 retained earnings overstated. The error is not discovered until 20X2, so it is still in retained earnings at the end of 20X1.

3. Understatement of beginning inventory

12/31/20X1 retained earningsNE = 1/1/20X1 retained earnings↓ + Net income↑ − Dividends
Note 3: 1/1/20X1 inventory is 12/31/20X0 inventory. The same error understated 20X0 ending inventory, which overstated 20X0 cost of goods sold and understated 20X0 net income, so 1/1/20X1 retained earnings is understated.
Note 4: The understatement in 1/1/20X1 retained earnings and the overstatement in 20X1 net income are the same amount and cancel, so 12/31/20X1 retained earnings has no error.

8. Understatement of purchases plus understatement of ending inventory by the same amount

Cost of goods sold
Beginning inventory + Purchases + Freight-in − Purchase returns and allowances − Cost of goods sold = Ending inventory
Cost of goods soldNE = Beginning inventory + Purchases↓ + Freight-in − Purchase returns and allowances − Ending inventory↓
Note 1: Purchases are added and ending inventory is subtracted. Understated purchases lower cost of goods sold, and understated ending inventory raises it by the same amount, so the two cancel.
Net income
Net incomeNE = Sales − Cost of goods soldNE − Other expenses
Note 2: Cost of goods sold has no error, so net income has no error.
Retained earnings
12/31/20X1 retained earningsNE = 1/1/20X1 retained earnings + Net incomeNE − Dividends
Note 3: 1/1/20X1 retained earnings, net income and dividends have no error, so 12/31/20X1 retained earnings has no error.
